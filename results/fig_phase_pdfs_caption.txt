Draft caption, Fig. 5 (figures/fig_phase_pdfs.pdf)

Density distributions of the neutral phases at four heights, over the 1 kpc x 1 kpc footprint. Columns show the CNM (a, d), UNM (b, e) and WNM (c, f), each on its own log10(n_H / cm^-3) range; the top row (a-c) is volume weighted and the bottom row (d-f) mass weighted. Each panel shows step lines of the distribution in 0.05 dex bins, one line per slab in shades of the phase colour: darkest for the midplane slab, lighter with height. Slabs are |z - z_c| <= 30 pc: at d = 0 the single slab centred on z_mid, and at d = 100, 200 and 300 pc the union of the slabs centred on z_mid - d and z_mid + d (above and below combined). Volume weights are cell counts (all cells are 2 pc cubes); mass weights are the sums of n_H over the cells in each bin (mass is proportional to n_H, since rho = 1.4 m_H n_H and every cell has the same volume). Every curve is normalised to unit area over log10 n_H, so the curves compare the shapes of the distributions, not their amounts; the y axis is in arbitrary units, as in Shelest et al. (2026, Fig. 3a). Phases are from the dP/dn classification used in Figs. 6 and 7 (Shelest et al. 2026): with I_UV the local radiation field, WNM is n_H < n_W,max(I_UV) and CNM is n_H > n_C,min(I_UV), the densities of the warm- and cold-branch turning points (dP/dn = 0) of the thermal-equilibrium curve, and UNM lies between them. Because the phases are defined by density thresholds, each distribution has sharp edges at the thresholds; the edges are not perfectly vertical because the thresholds are evaluated at each cell's own I_UV. Hot ionised cells (HIM) are flagged separately and not drawn, as their n_H is not physical. The amounts not shown by the unit-area curves are as follows. Volume fractions of the slab (all cells, HIM included) for the CNM, UNM, WNM and HIM: 0.019, 0.173, 0.354 and 0.453 at d = 0; 0.009, 0.075, 0.327 and 0.589 at d = 100 pc; 0.0018, 0.025, 0.250 and 0.723 at d = 200 pc; and 0.0002, 0.0095, 0.150 and 0.840 at d = 300 pc. Fractions of the neutral (CNM + UNM + WNM) mass, with the HIM excluded because its n_H is not physical, for the CNM, UNM and WNM: 0.257, 0.527 and 0.216 at d = 0; 0.256, 0.436 and 0.308 at d = 100 pc; 0.118, 0.367 and 0.516 at d = 200 pc; and 0.036, 0.300 and 0.664 at d = 300 pc. The median log10 n_H (16-84% range; read from the 0.05 dex bins) of the CNM, UNM and WNM is, volume weighted, 0.83 (0.68-1.08), 0.23 (-0.03-0.48) and -0.48 (-0.78 - -0.18) at d = 0 and 0.83 (0.73-1.03), 0.03 (-0.08-0.28) and -0.88 (-1.08 - -0.53) at d = 300 pc; mass weighted, 0.98 (0.73-1.28), 0.38 (0.13-0.63) and -0.28 (-0.58 - -0.08) at d = 0 and 0.93 (0.73-1.23), 0.13 (-0.03-0.43) and -0.63 (-0.98 - -0.33) at d = 300 pc. The width of the d = 0 CNM distribution, sigma_s = 0.501 (the volume-weighted standard deviation of ln(n_H/<n_H>), Fig. 6), compares with sigma_s = 0.485 of Shelest et al. (2026) for |z| <= 30 pc; our slab is centred on z_mid = -11.9 pc rather than on z = 0. Every phase has at least 100 cells in every slab, so no curve is omitted.
